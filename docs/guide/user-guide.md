# OneEditor — user guide

OneEditor edits video, sound and animation for Cambridge International AS & A Level IT (9626),
syllabus sections 11 and 20.1. It has three editors, chosen at the top left: **Video**, **Audio**
and **Animation**. The theory is in the **Learn** menu at the top of the screen.

> Rest the mouse on any button to see what it does. Most tools also have a **Why use this?** link:
> rest the mouse on it (or click it to keep it open) to see the reason the tool is used, in the
> words the exam expects. Escape or a click elsewhere closes it.

## Saving and moving your work

- **File ▸ Save** makes a project file (`.mve`). If you used Audio mode, a folder called
  "_project name_ audio" is made next to it with your sound in it. Keep the two together.
- Your videos, pictures and music are **not** copied into the project: they stay where they are.
  To take a project to another computer or a USB stick, use **File ▸ Package Project…**, choose an
  empty folder, and copy that whole folder. Open the `.mve` inside it on the other computer
  (Windows or Mac).
- **Export** makes the finished video or sound file. Export does not save the project: save it too.
- OneEditor saves your work in the background. If it closes unexpectedly, it offers to restore your
  work the next time it opens.
- **In an exam**, open **File ▸ Candidate Details…** and type your centre number, candidate number
  and name once. Every Save and Export box then suggests the name the paper asks for, for example
  `Gas_ZZ999_9999`: change only the first part. Click **Clear** there before someone else uses the
  computer.

### Arrange the panels

Drag the edge between a panel and the work area to make the panel wider or narrower (or the
timeline taller); double-click the edge for its usual size. The small arrow on the edge hides the
panel, and the thin strip left behind shows it again. **View ▸ Show / Hide Left Panel**, **Right
Panel** and **Timeline** do the same, and **View ▸ Reset Layout** puts everything back. OneEditor
remembers your layout.

## Video mode

### 1. Set up the project

Click the chip at the top (for example **16:9 · 1920 × 1080 · 30 fps**) to choose the **aspect
ratio**, the **resolution** and the **frame rate**, and how clips of another shape fit (black bars,
crop to fill, or stretch). Set this first: the coursebook tasks always say which size to use.

### 2. Bring in your media

**Import** (or **File ▸ Import Media…**) adds videos, pictures and sound to the **media bin** on the
left. Click **Add** on an item to put it at the end of the timeline.

### 3. Edit on the timeline

| To… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Select a clip | Click it. Its settings appear in the **inspector** on the right (tabs: Clip, Audio, Effects, Text). |
| Trim the start or end | Drag the clip's left or right edge, or type the **Start point** / **End point** in the Clip tab. |
| Split a clip in two | Move the playhead (click the ruler) and click **Split**. |
| Join two pieces again | Select the first one and click **Join**. |
| Change the order | Drag a clip to another place on the main track. |
| Delete a clip | Select it and click **Delete** (or press <kbd>Delete</kbd>). |
| Make a clip faster or slower | Clip tab ▸ **Speed**: a preset, or type any speed from 0.25× to 4× (such as 1.25). |
| Show a picture for longer | Select it ▸ **Duration**. |
| Make a picture fit the video's shape | Media bin ▸ **Resize & crop…** on the picture: **Crop to fit** keeps its shape, **Stretch to fit** changes its shape and cuts nothing off. |
| Put a picture on another picture | Media bin ▸ **Place a picture on it…** on the background picture: choose the picture to place, its width (% of the background), one of nine places and a margin. It is saved as a new picture. |

**Snap** makes dragged edges jump to the playhead and to other clips, so clips line up exactly.

### 4. Text: titles, captions, subtitles and credits

- **Title** adds a text slide to the main track (on a colour, a picture or a video).
- **Caption** and **Subtitle** add words on top of the video at the playhead.
- **Credits** add the names of the people and the sources; tick **Scroll up the screen** for
  scrolling credits, and leave blank lines between the parts.

In the **Text** tab:

- **Font** opens a list of 26 fonts in groups, each written in its own letters. Use the same font
  for all the words in a video unless the task says otherwise.
- **Ready-made looks** (Neon, Gold, Fire, Comic, Highlighter…) change the colours and effects in one
  click. **Readability and effects** has a shadow, an outline, a box, glow, gradient and 3D, and a
  second colour. The note under it says whether the words are easy to read.
- **Position**: the 3 × 3 grid (for example top right), and left / centre / right lines.
- **Effects**: fade in and out, an **Entrance** (fly in, rise, drop and bounce, zoom, pop, slam
  down and shake, type on, spin in, whip in) and an **Exit** (fly out, sink, zoom away, pop out,
  spin away, whip off).

### 5. Effects and transitions

In a clip's **Effects** tab:

- **Fade** in and out, to black or to white.
- **Filters & colour**: brightness, contrast, saturation, black and white, sepia, blur and more.
- **Pan & zoom**: move across or into a still picture.
- **Transition into this clip**: 43 transitions in five groups. Rest the mouse on a tile to see it
  move. Choose how long it lasts underneath.

### 6. Sound in a video

- A video clip's **Audio** tab: change the clip's **volume**, **Remove sound**, or **Separate sound**
  onto its own track.
- **Add music** puts a sound file on the music track at the playhead. A voiceover goes on the
  second track. Drag sound clips to move them; select one to change its volume, fades and speed.
  Tick **Lower under voiceover** on the music to make the words easier to hear.
- **Sound FX** opens the built-in sound effects (a whoosh, a ding, a drum roll…): **Play** to hear
  one, **Add** to put it at the playhead on the **Effects 1** track (or **Effects 2** if that is
  busy). Move, trim and change its volume like music.

### 7. Snapshot (a still from the video)

Move the playhead to the frame you want and click **Snapshot**. Step frame by frame with **Previous
frame** / **Next frame**, type a **File name** (for example `Bali1`), choose the picture format,
and choose whether to hide the text and put the still on the timeline.

### 8. Export

**Export** opens the export window:

- **Destination** presets: Internet / YouTube, mobile phone (medium or small), DVD player (PAL or
  NTSC), Blu-ray / HD television — or **Custom**.
- **Format**: MP4, MOV, AVI, WMV, MPG and more, each with its advantages and disadvantages.
- **Resolution** (also *same as* each video on the timeline, for example 852 × 480, or **Custom size…**),
  **frame rate** and **quality**, with the **estimated file size**.

## Audio mode

Audio mode edits sound on **tracks**, like Audacity.

### Tracks and clips

- **Import audio** opens a sound file (MP3, WAV, M4A, AAC, FLAC, OGG, or the sound of a video) as a
  new track. **Tracks ▸ Add new mono / stereo track** adds an empty one. **Tracks ▸ Add a sound
  effect…** adds a built-in sound effect as a new track at the cursor.
- Each track holds **clips**. With the **Clip tool** (the arrows button, chosen at the start):
  - click a clip to select it (the playhead goes where you clicked);
  - drag a clip to move it in time;
  - drag a clip's edge to trim it (drag it back out to get the sound back);
  - **Split** cuts the selected clip at the playhead; **Join** puts it back together with the clip
    right after it; **Delete** removes the selected clip and leaves a silent gap.
  - the side panel shows the selected clip's **Starts at**: type a time (such as 8.25) to move it
    there exactly.
- With the **Selection tool** (the I-beam), drag across the sound to select part of it. Then use
  **Cut**, **Copy**, **Paste**, **Trim to selection**, **Insert silence** or an effect on just that
  part. You can also type the **Selection start** and **Selection end** at the bottom.
- Each track has **Mute**, **Solo**, **Gain** (louder or quieter) and **Pan** (left / right).
- **Tracks** menu: move tracks up and down, align them end to end or to the cursor, **Mix stereo down
  to mono**, **Mix and render** several tracks into one, remove tracks.

### Effects

Select the sound (a clip, part of it, or everything with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd>),
then choose from the **Effect** menu: Amplify, Normalise, Compressor, Fade in, Fade out, Change
speed, Change pitch, Echo, Delay, Reverb, Graphic EQ, High-pass filter, Low-pass filter, Noise
reduction. Most open a window with settings and a **Preview** button.

**Noise reduction** has two steps, as in Audacity: first select a part with **only** the
background noise and click **Get noise profile**; then select the sound to clean and open Noise
reduction again to apply it.

### Recording a voiceover

Click **Record** (the red button). Choose the microphone and watch the level meter, then start:
after a 3-second count-in, the other tracks play while you speak (overdubbing). Press
<kbd>Space</kbd> or **Stop** to finish: your voice becomes a new track at the playhead.

### Export

**Export** (or **File ▸ Export Audio…**): MP3, M4A, AAC, OGG, WAV or FLAC; sample rate, bit rate, mono
or stereo, and tags. It shows the estimated size next to the same sound as a WAV.

## Animation mode

An animation is its own file (`.mvea`): **File ▸ New Animation**, **Open Animation…**, and Save
while Animation mode is showing. **File ▸ Package Project…** in Animation mode copies the animation
and its pictures into one folder, to hand in or take to another computer.

### 1. Set up the stage

Click the chip at the top (for example **3:2 · 600 × 400 · 24 fps**) to set the stage's **width
and height** in pixels (or an aspect ratio preset: 4:3, 16:9, 16:10, 2.35:1), its **colour** (or
**Transparent**: no background, shown as a checkerboard), the **frame rate** and the **length**. Only what is on the stage is in the animation; (0, 0) is its
top-left corner.

The tool strip beside the stage has **Rulers** (drag a guide out of a ruler), **Grid** and
**Snap** (to the grid, guides and other objects; hold <kbd>Alt</kbd> to move freely).

### 2. Draw and place objects

| To… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Draw a shape | **Rectangle**, **Ellipse**, **Star**, **Polygon** or **Line**, then drag on the stage (<kbd>Shift</kbd> for a square or circle). |
| Draw a path | **Pen**: click points, double-click or <kbd>Enter</kbd> to finish (click the first point to close it). **Brush**: draw freehand. |
| Add words | **Text**, click the stage, type in the Properties panel. **Split into letters** gives each letter its own object. |
| Add a picture | **Picture**, click the stage, choose a PNG, JPG, GIF, BMP or SVG. **Trace bitmap…** turns it into vector shapes. |
| Add a button | **Button** (Pause, Continue, Restart or Go to frame) for the web page. |
| Set exact numbers | The **Properties** panel: X, Y, W, H, rotation, opacity, fill (solid or gradient) and outline. |
| Make one object of several | Select them, **Group** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd>); **Ungroup** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>⇧</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd>). |

### Ready-made pictures: the Library

**Library** on the tool strip (or <kbd>K</kbd>) has cartoons (a child, animals, vehicles, a tree, a
house…), icons and symbols (arrows, ticks and crosses, speech bubbles, weather, flowchart shapes)
and backgrounds (sky and grass, underwater, night, sunset, a classroom, a city). Search or choose a
category, then click one: a picture goes in the middle of the stage; a background fills the stage on
a layer of its own, behind everything. Each is a group of named shapes — Ungroup it to recolour or
animate a part, such as a car's wheels.

### 3. Layers

Each layer has its own timeline; the top layer is in front. In the **Layers** panel: **New layer**,
**New folder**, move up and down, rename (double-click), **Hide**, **Lock** (the padlock) and
**Mask** — a mask's shapes show only the part of the layer beneath that they cover (lock the mask
layer to see it work).

### 4. Animate: keyframes and tweens

Move the playhead (click the timeline's ruler, or type a frame or a time such as `3 s` in **Go
to…**), then change the object — drag it, or type a new value. That makes a **keyframe** there,
and OneEditor works out every frame in between (a **tween**). Click a keyframe dot to choose its
**easing**, **Hold** it, **Repeat** or **Reverse** it.

- **Motion path**: draw a path, then select the object ▸ Properties ▸ **Follow path** (tick
  **Orient to path** to turn with it).
- **Morph** (shape tween): Properties ▸ **Morph into** another shape at the playhead.
- **Effects**: select objects, move the playhead to where it should start, then Properties ▸
  **Effects**: choose one (fade in or out, fly in or out, zoom, bounce in, spin, pulse, shake,
  wobble, typewriter for text, blink), type how long it **lasts**, and **Add effect**. It only adds
  keyframes, so you can change them on the timeline; Undo takes it away.
- **Loop** and **Stop** on the timeline; **Test** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd>) plays the
  animation as its web page, where its buttons work.

### 5. Stop motion and frame-by-frame

- **Stop motion**: name your photographs in order (frame01, frame02…) and put them in one folder.
  Click **Import frames** on the tool strip, choose how many pictures a second (12 is usual), then
  **Choose folder…**. They go on a new layer, each held for its frames; the stage is made longer if
  needed.
- **Frame-by-frame** (as a flip book): on the timeline click **Frame-by-frame** to make the current
  layer one. Move the playhead and click **Duplicate frame** (<kbd>F6</kbd>) to copy the drawing and
  change it a little, or **Insert blank keyframe** (<kbd>F7</kbd>) to start an empty one. **Onion
  skin** shows the drawings before and after faintly. Remove a keyframe with the delete button (or
  <kbd>⇧</kbd>+<kbd>F6</kbd>).

### 6. Export

**Export** (or **File ▸ Export Animation…**): an **MP4 video**, an **animated GIF** (loops), an
**HTML web animation** (one file that plays in any browser, with its buttons), a **PNG sequence**
(one picture per frame), a **PNG picture of this frame** or an **SVG picture of this frame** (vector
shapes, sharp at any size). With a transparent stage the PNG, GIF and web page are see-through; an
MP4 is drawn on white. The dialog shows the size, length and
advantages of each. **Send to Video mode** puts the exported MP4 on the video timeline.

## Learn mode

Open it from the **Learn** menu in the menu bar. It fills the window; the tabs at the top switch
between its pages, and **Back to …** returns to the editor you were using. **Learn ▸ Glossary**
opens over any editor.

- **Inspector & sampling**: drop or choose any media file to see what is inside it (container,
  codecs, lossy or lossless, resolution, aspect ratio, bit rates, sample rate, channels…). Click a
  row to learn what it means. The **audio file-size calculator** works out a file's size step by
  step, and the **sampling visualiser** shows how sample rate and resolution change the sound that
  is stored.
- **Formats & compression**: the format guide, and real comparisons of the same sound or video saved
  in different formats, with their sizes, to play side by side.
- **Animation**: the types of animation with their advantages and disadvantages, the principles
  (frames, key frames, timings, tweening), the components and animation variables, and the same
  motion at 5, 12 and 24 fps side by side.
- **Task checker**: your teacher gives you a `.mvetask` file; open it and your export to see whether
  it has the right format, size, length, frame rate, sound and looping. For a PNG sequence, select
  all its pictures.
- **Glossary**: the coursebooks' key words.

## Coursebook tasks, step by step

**Trim a clip and remove its sound** (for example Book B Task 11b): add the clip; in the Clip tab
type the **Start point** and **End point**; in the Audio tab tick **Remove sound**; Export.

**Change the speed** (Task 11c): Clip tab ▸ **Speed**, for example 1.5×.

**A title over a picture** (Task 11a): **Title**; in the Text tab type the words and choose the font,
size and colour; under Background choose **Picture**; set the **Duration**; choose an Entrance.

**Scrolling credits** (Book A 11.14): **Credits**; type the names with blank lines between the
parts; tick **Scroll up the screen**; set the duration.

**Cut sound from a point to the end** (Book B 11.2.4): Audio mode; with the Selection tool type
the **Selection start** and drag (or type) to the end; **Cut** (or <kbd>Delete</kbd>).

**Practice paper (the Bali video), question 1**: select the noise-only start ▸ Effect ▸ Noise
reduction ▸ **Get noise profile**; select all ▸ Noise reduction ▸ Apply. Select from 8 s to the end
▸ **Amplify** (the suggested gain is the most without clipping). Select 0–2 s ▸ <kbd>Delete</kbd>.
Put the cursor at 17.7 s ▸ **Insert silence** 7.7 s. Select each fade's part ▸ Fade in / Fade out.
Select from 37.3 s to the end ▸ <kbd>Delete</kbd>. Export as MP3, stereo, 44 100 Hz.

**A star that turns once** (workbook Chapter 7, Task 1): stage chip ▸ 600 × 400; **Star**, then
type X 128, Y 102, W 135, H 135 in Properties; playhead to `3 s` (frame 72 at 24 fps) ▸ Rotation
360; Export ▸ MP4.

**Pause and Continue buttons** (Task 4): **Button** twice; set one to **Pause** and one to
**Continue** in Properties; **Test** to try them; Export ▸ HTML web animation.

**A stop-motion clip** (coursebook 19.1): photographs in one folder ▸ **Import frames** ▸ 12
pictures a second ▸ Choose folder…; Export ▸ MP4.

## If something goes wrong

- **A file is missing** (it was moved or renamed): its item in the media bin says so; click
  **Locate…** and find it. Use **Package Project** next time you move a project.
- **A picture in an animation is missing**: it shows as a dashed box; open the animation from the
  folder the pictures are in, or use Package Project next time.
- **An export fails**: the message says why in plain words. Your project is not changed. Try
  another format or a smaller size, and make sure there is free space on the disk.
- **The microphone does not work**: see "Microphone" in the install guide (`INSTALL.md`), then close
  the recording window and open it again.
- **Something else went wrong** ("Something went wrong", a frozen window, OneEditor closed by
  itself): choose **Help ▸ Report a Problem…**, save the report (on the Desktop), write in it what
  you were doing, and give it to your teacher. It lists the last things OneEditor did and the errors
  it met; nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Help ▸ Welcome** shows the first page again. **Help ▸ Licences** lists the free software
  OneEditor includes.
